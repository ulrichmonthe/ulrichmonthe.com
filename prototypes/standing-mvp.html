How the verdict is computed

Every figure above is derived from Form 990-PF, the annual return every US private foundation files. Part XV line 3a lists grants paid with recipient names; Part VIII lists officers and trustees. Comparing grantee lists across consecutive filing years yields the new-grantee rate — the share of a year's grants going to organizations the foundation had not funded in the prior three years.

That single number does work the "accepts unsolicited proposals" checkbox cannot. The checkbox is self-reported and never audited. The grant ledger is behavior. A foundation reporting an open door with a 1.8% new-grantee rate is telling you two different things, and only one of them is evidence.

Where grantee names will not resolve at a high enough rate, the tool returns no verdict and says why. Refusal is a supported output, not a failure state — an unresolved name looks exactly like a first-time grantee, which would make a closed foundation read as open.

Feasibility, checked. The grant schedule is a mandatory repeating element in the 990-PF e-file schema — recipient name, address, purpose and amount — and the Taxpayer First Act eliminated paper filing for all private foundations for tax years ending on or after 31 July 2020. Coverage for this window is effectively complete.

The live risk is different from the expected one. Part XV carries no recipient EIN, so every comparison is a name match. That is tractable within one foundation's own filing series, but match quality varies by preparer — and per-filer match rates on real data are the number that decides whether this works. It cannot be known without running it.
